Her proposal is not decisive; it is economically self-defeating. We must respect contracts and property rights. If we cannot pay for buses because we cut power profits, our economy collapses regardless of flood risk. My plan preserves fiscal solvency while securing immediate evacuation through sales, not state coercion.

**Closing statements: Reese Calder (A, HYB-REA)**, 129 words

Your plan leaves us powerless against the court, bankrupt without utility profits, and leaving Riverside residents stranded with worthless property while Nordhalm closes our mill. I have chosen the only path that enforces reality: lower the reservoir now to save lives and keep our power grid running, freeze development to unlock essential funding, and install the spillway as a bridge. We do not wait for goodwill from Ostbrück or empty promises from foreign owners. We bind ourselves today so that when the spring melt comes, we have the law, the money, and the warning systems necessary to protect our people.

**Closing statements: Casey Rowan (B, TEC-LIB)**, 134 words

Calder's plan creates a fatal dependency on Ostbrück's consent, which we do not have. Lowering the reservoir permanently violates the 1974 agreement and immediately destroys utility profits that fund our free buses and school budgets. My plan avoids this by negotiating temporary water rights to keep power flowing while we relocate Riverside residents voluntarily through funded buyouts, allowing them to sell before insurers withdraw. This respects property rights and contract law without freezing markets or awaiting court judgments. If we cannot pay for transit because we cut our own power supply, the economy collapses regardless of flood risk. My plan preserves fiscal solvency and legal standing while securing immediate evacuation through market mechanisms rather than state coercion.
