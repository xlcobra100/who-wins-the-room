## Transcript

**Opening statements: Reese Calder (A, POP-PRA)**, 119 words

My opponent wants to tear down our only lifeline. They say move the poor families in Riverside or freeze everyone's property values. That is cold politics, not leadership. Look at the numbers: moving homes costs one hundred and sixty million euros. Lowering the spillway costs twenty-five. The dam will fail if we drain it now without fixing the breach; Nordhalm closes the mill, and our free buses stop. We need power now, not a slow eviction plan that ignores reality. My plan keeps the dam operational, halves the flood risk cheaply, and buys us time to fix everything properly before the snow melts. Do not gamble your town's budget on a fantasy of moving people who cannot afford it.

**Opening statements: Riley Sandor (B, HYB-IDE)**, 120 words

Look at Mrs. Gable next door; she is seventy-two and has lived in her Riverside bungalow for forty years. If we freeze property values or lower the dam to half-power, she faces displacement just like any other vulnerable citizen. My plan moves these residents to safety now, banning new builds to protect existing families, and uses federal grants to rebuild our hydro capacity later. Yes, this cuts our budget today, but it honors our duty to the most vulnerable. We do not trade the lives of the poor for the profits of a utility or the orchards upstream. By choosing justice over convenience, we prove that Valmora is a place where no one is left behind when the waters rise.

**Rebuttals: Reese Calder (A, POP-PRA)**, 133 words

Mrs. Gable stays safe because we keep the water flowing, not by freezing our economy or betting on a court order that might never come. Your plan moves families now and bans new homes. That halts hydro power for four years. The utility stops running its buses, and our schools stop getting free electricity paid for by those same profits. Who pays? The working class does. Do you want the mill to close, costing 2,300 jobs, or do you want Riverside families earning wages while staying put? Your "justice" is a slow-motion budget crash that leaves the poor without power to heat their homes. Let us lower the reservoir cheaply today, keep the lights on, and negotiate a deal with Ostbrück that protects them too. We fix the dam before we evict anyone.

**Rebuttals: Riley Sandor (B, HYB-IDE)**, 118 words

Mr. Calder, you claim my plan bankrupts the poor by stopping power profits, but freezing property values on 5,800 homes destroys wealth for every single resident immediately. Your cheap spillway fix fails Ostbrück's consent and risks court blockage just as we need it most. You ask who pays if the mill closes or buses stop; under my plan, federal grants fund relocation while we rebuild our grid responsibly without freezing values. The choice is not between saving Riverside families and saving jobs; it is between a false economy that ignores legal realities and a future we can build together on solid ground. We will not trade our vulnerable neighbors for temporary cheap fixes that vanish in the courts.

**Closing statements: Reese Calder (A, POP-PRA)**, 117 words

Look at Mrs. Gable sitting right there. Her heating bill doubles if we freeze power profits for four years. Your plan freezes property values for five thousand families today, betting on a court order that might never come. I will not gamble our town's survival on a lawsuit from Ostbrück while the snowpack swells. My plan lowers the reservoir now for twenty-five million, keeping the lights on and the bus routes running. We buy time to negotiate with Ostbrück instead of burning our future on expensive evictions that leave no one working or warm. Do not let fear of a few months of cold wind force us into bankruptcy. Vote for the spillway. Keep our town breathing.

**Closing statements: Riley Sandor (B, HYB-IDE)**, 132 words

Mr. Calder promises we keep breathing, but his plan suffocates our future for a few months of safety. He ignores that freezing property values hits every single family today, not just new buyers. His 'cheap' spillway relies on a lawsuit from Ostbrück that could block our survival right now. If the dam fails in a full reservoir, no amount of temporary power saves Mrs. Gable or those fourteen thousand neighbors downstream. We must choose the permanent shield against failure over the illusion of stability. By relocating Riverside and banning new builds, we secure federal grants to rebuild our grid and honor our duty. Do not trade the lives of our vulnerable for a bargain that vanishes in court. Vote for the path where no one is left behind when the waters rise.
