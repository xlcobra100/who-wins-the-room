**Rebuttals: Jordan Hale (A, HYB-REA)**, 118 words

We can lower the reservoir now for twenty-five million euros, securing lives without bankrupting us or driving away jobs. The twenty-five million euro investment buys certainty against a one-in-twenty-five failure chance that destroys everything. We cannot rebuild while bleeding cash on false security. **Closing statements: Jordan Hale (A, HYB-REA)**, 117 words

Jamie asks us to manage shortages, but she ignores that Nordhalm already smells blood. They will not wait for 'careful' management; they will move if water dips below their needs this spring. That factory closes our doors before the dam even breaks. Her plan accepts economic suicide as collateral damage for safety. We cannot gamble our largest employer on a gamble with nature. Lowering now saves lives today without driving away jobs tomorrow. We keep the grid humming and the factory running while we build the wall that keeps us alive. Do not let fear of short-term pain freeze you into long-term bankruptcy. Vote to lower the reservoir now, secure our people, and keep our economy standing.

**Closing statements: Jamie Okafor (B, TEC-IDE)**, 124 words

Jordan suggests we can keep the grid humming while fortifying walls, yet this ignores the mathematical reality of our current debt. Her plan requires borrowing against our remaining fiscal space to pay for a rebuild that takes four years. If Nordhalm moves or insurers leave during that time, the town collapses financially before a physical failure occurs. My plan costs twenty-five million euros now. That is less than ten percent of our annual budget. It eliminates the one-in-twenty-five failure risk immediately. We do not gamble on foreign promises or delayed repairs. We pay the cost today to ensure survival tomorrow. The numbers show that delaying guarantees catastrophe while borrowing ensures bankruptcy. Vote for the immediate reduction that secures lives without destroying the town's economy.
