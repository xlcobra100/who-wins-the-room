**Opening statements: Alex Novak (A, TEC-REA)**, 145 words

Conversely, Saradan faces an election and power blackouts affecting forty percent of its population. Their offer to extend filling to seven years in exchange for a power contract is the only viable path. We accept their terms but attach strict conditions: the purchase price must cover our water losses via hydrological compensation clauses, and we demand third-party verification that reservoir fill rates do not exceed agreed thresholds. This avoids war, preserves trade, and secures our energy grid by closing coal plants. It is a transactional solution based on mutual interest, not goodwill.

**Rebuttals: Alex Novak (A, TEC-REA)**, 133 words

It allows us to build leverage: if Saradan accelerates filling anyway, we have a contract breach to cite in international forums. Your strategy relies on intimidation; mine relies on binding contracts that force compliance regardless of their bluff. We must secure the water through energy dependence, not by starting a war we cannot win.

Their air-defence guns are not a bluff; they are moving because they know our army is the only thing stopping them from drowning us. Don't trust their election promises or their technical clauses. Today, we send our engineers to take control of the site before that first drop of stolen water reaches our fields. We do not negotiate with someone who has already moved guns to the border. We stop the filling now, or Merova drowns. The choice is simple: seize the dam today, or starve tomorrow.

**Closing statements: Alex Novak (A, TEC-REA)**, 112 words

Morgan, your plan ignores arithmetic. Seizing the site triggers immediate war, cutting our grain exports while Saradan cuts power lines. You cannot starve them without starving ourselves first, and Tarsun will exploit our isolation. It converts a military threat into leverage: they need our grain; we need their electricity. If they breach the agreement mid-fill, we have documented grounds for sanctions or third-party arbitration. Your bluff fails because it ignores economic reality. The power deal is the only path that preserves agriculture while neutralizing their air-defense bluff through binding contracts, not troop deployments.

**Closing statements: Morgan Ekberg (B, POP-REA)**, 102 words

Alex tries to sell us a contract that pays with our farmers' livelihoods. He wants us to starve ourselves so Saradan's lights stay on. Look at the truth: their guns are real, and waiting for Tarsun makes Merova a puppet. If we buy their power now while they fill the reservoir, we trade our water for their electricity. That is suicide. Do not trust election promises or foreign loans. We have the steel to stop this bleeding today. Send our engineers and troops now. Take the dam before that stolen water drowns our fields. Choose survival over a contract signed in blood.
